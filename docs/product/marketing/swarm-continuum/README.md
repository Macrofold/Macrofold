# Swarm Continuum

The separate collection at `/swarm-continuum/index.html` expands [Metamorphosis](../swarm-metamorphosis/README.md) with additional related sections, a curved incoming lattice, and broader continuous loss of order. Every earlier collection remains preserved.

## Structure and formation

The inlet is a shallow three-dimensional saddle with curved rows, changing width, and unequal edges. The rows gradually loosen across a broad curved region before gathering into evolving surfaces. There is no single horizontal coordinate where all particles switch behavior.

The population increases from 40,000 to 60,000 rounded particles. Additional outer regions expand the assemblies while their existing sections keep morphing; higher minimum separation reduces contraction toward the center. Hypercell develops three offset families with more particles tracing its edges. Five organic and five polyhedral studies remain available one at a time. Alveoli and Rhizome replace Aeolian and Meissner in this collection.

These are art-directed procedural surfaces and shared movement fields, not a physical neighbor-force simulation. Particle identities remain stable and move independently through the changing material coordinates. No invisible solid or depth buffer occludes their surfaces. Orthographic projection keeps sprite diameter and brightness independent of camera distance; adaptive resolution retains the full population.

## Playback

The loop remains twenty seconds. Organization begins around two seconds, the structure is established by six, and morphing continues until fifteen. Staggered wind release retains momentum and disperses the structure by nineteen, followed by one empty second.

The studio starts paused at nine seconds. Drag the timeline and press **Play** to resume from that point. **Play formation** runs zero to six seconds. The **Playback speed** slider spans 1.0–2.0× in 0.1 increments. Changing speed preserves the current position; selection, pause, and scrubbing preserve the chosen rate. Timeline labels refer to animation time.
